## What Actually Affects Your Price

A handful of factors drive most of the cost variation we see on Liberty Hill roofs:

**Roof size and pitch.** Bigger roofs cost more, obviously, but steep-pitch roofs also require more labor, safety equipment, and time — which adds to the bill even on smaller homes.

**Material selection.** Standard three-tab shingles are the most affordable option, architectural shingles sit in the middle, and metal roofing costs more upfront but lasts two to three times longer. Our [materials & cost hub](https://www.rippleroofs.com/hubs/materials-cost) walks through this trade-off in more detail.

**Tear-off requirements.** Removing one existing layer of shingles is standard. If a previous owner installed a second layer without removing the first (common in older Liberty Hill homes), tear-off costs increase.

**Decking repairs.** Once the old shingles come off, we sometimes find soft or rotted plywood decking underneath, especially around chimneys, vents, and valleys. Replacing damaged decking is priced separately and isn't something we can always predict until tear-off begins.

**Ventilation and flashing upgrades.** Older homes often have inadequate attic ventilation, which shortens shingle life. Adding proper ventilation during a replacement is a smart long-term investment — read more in our [attic ventilation guide](https://www.rippleroofs.com/services/attic-ventilation).

## Repair or Replace? Quick Signs to Watch For

Not every roof problem means a full roof replacement. Here's a quick gut check:

- **Repair territory:** isolated leaks, a handful of missing shingles, minor flashing issues, roof under 15 years old — see our [roof repair services](https://www.rippleroofs.com/services/roof-repairs)
- **Replacement territory:** widespread granule loss, curling or cracking shingles across the roof, multiple leak points, visible sagging, or roof age over 20 years
